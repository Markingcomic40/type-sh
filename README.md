# TypeSH

Minimal CLI typing test.

> Note: This project is, first and foremost, a Rust learning exercise. It’s also something of a small getaway from the relentless presence of AI in this new era that has come upon us.
I am quite conflicted with it. On one hand, its capabilities are quite astonishing and on the other, it can be bafflingly stupid with a confidence that borders on theatrical with some matters. Like goddamn, maybe we just expect too much of it too so small mistakes end up seeming worse than they are but lmfao..... Idk. That tension is both fascinating and, occasionally, unsettling. I am by no means anti-AI. In fact, I began programming because I was drawn to AI. Every meaningful advancement in that space still excites me and sparks my curiosity. What I struggle with are the extremes: the camp that treats it as a cursed instrument to be avoided at all costs, and the camp that proclaims it the ultimate solution and the meaning of life, the kind that makes linkdn even worse a place to be than it already was; as though vibing away alone were the future of craftsmanship. WHICH IT MAY BE BUT LIKE GODDAMN CHILL CAN WE TALK ABOUT SOMETHIN ELSE FFS. 
To me, as a tool, it’s amazing. Code completion alone is honestly addicting and I'm a huge huge fan, and automatic Git message generation are frkin amazing, sometimes they suck tho... but it takes away that had to name shit issue lmfao. It's super cool for exploring, for accelerating understanding, for lowering the barrier to entry. Sure. But, I find myself fatigued by its omnipresence. There’s something about the current atmosphere that feels different, less earned, less... I dont know. Sometimes it also kinda just gaslights the heck out of you into thinking its got this amazing design that you follow and later on as you gain experience realize gosh this is so shit and if I read the docs a bit more or whatever maybe I would've realized but then again maybe you would've made the same mistake.
It’s conflicting. Genuinely. But either way.... Back to the main point...
This project is also a reset. I’m not trying to build the next billion-dollar SaaS. It’s not revolutionary. It’s simply something I find interesting and enjoyable. A space to learn, to tinker, and to reconnect with the feeling that made me suddenly switch to this domain in the first place that made me ig love programming.
I’ve been building a game in Rust recently, but it grew complicated quickly, especially with Bevy and the ecosystem around it. I wanted to step back from the weight of a full game engine and focus on something more contained, more deliberate.
So here’s the constraint: no AI for implementation. At most, I’ll consult it for documentation clarifications or general best practices if the docs are painfully opaque and unnecessarily mumbo jumbo, but no generated code, no copy-paste, no vibes. Not that this is my day to day life now, not that Im constnatly pressured by AI but I guess i just hear it around so much that its tiring and I felt like stepping back, just 30 minutes a day to a world where I forget it exists.
It will likely take far longer—mostly cause im mostly working when i commute but ig also cause ill have to debug without the handy "summarize the logs". The code may be worse than it could have been. I’m new to Rust; there will be friction, detours, and inefficiencies.
But that’s fine.
This is for fun. Thats it. Alr idk why i bothered writing this reflection, ill likely write a blog where i explore my thoughts deeper and write it down once i finish a v1 but GODDAMIT FIRST DAY SPENT ON THE README WTF. SHoudlve just let AI write this hahaha....... fml
